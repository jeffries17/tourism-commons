# CBT Monitoring System: Why This Matters

## 1. The Big Picture: Community Tourism as Essential Infrastructure

Community-Based Tourism (CBT) isn’t a side project – it’s essential to building a sustainable, inclusive tourism economy. When communities are engaged, supported, and visible, they become active partners in tourism development. That creates benefits far beyond a few popular destinations:

- **Economic diversification**: Tourism revenue reaches communities outside the main hotspots.
- **Cultural preservation**: Communities maintain and share their traditions on their own terms.
- **Local ownership**: Communities have a say in how tourism develops in their areas.
- **Long-term resilience**: Tourism becomes a community asset, not just a seasonal activity.

For that to work, communities need **consistent support, visibility, and connection** with tourism authorities. This system is designed to protect that connection.

---

## 2. The Challenge: Staying Connected at Scale

As tourism authorities work with dozens of communities across multiple regions, maintaining regular contact becomes increasingly hard.

**Without a system:**

- Staff rely on memory, WhatsApp chats, and scattered spreadsheets.
- Some communities get regular attention; others quietly disappear from focus.
- It’s difficult to see which communities:
  - Haven’t been visited or called in a while,
  - Have news to share (festivals, new products, new accommodation), or
  - Need support but haven’t reached out.
- Reporting upwards takes days of manual counting and guesswork.
- Communities can feel forgotten when contact is irregular.

**The result:** communities that could be strong partners don’t get the attention they deserve, and authorities have a weaker case when arguing that CBT is central to tourism strategy.

---

## 3. How This System Helps: Regular Touchpoints, Better Outcomes

### 3.1 Maintains Connection, Even When Everyone Is Busy

The system ensures no community goes unnoticed. By tracking **when each community was last contacted**, staff can see at a glance which communities need a check‑in.

This isn’t about micromanaging staff. It’s about making sure every community knows:

> “Someone is paying attention to us. We’re part of the story.”

**Why this matters:** communities that feel connected are more likely to:

- Share news about festivals, events, and new tourism products.
- Ask for help early when they face challenges.
- Participate actively in joint promotions and campaigns.
- Trust tourism authorities as long-term partners.

---

### 3.2 Surfaces Opportunities Before They’re Missed

When a community has news – a new homestay, a cultural event, a new trail – it’s easy for that information to stay local if no one asks.

Regular touchpoints mean staff can:

- Spot **content opportunities** (stories, videos, posts) early.
- Connect communities with **tour operators, media, and influencers**.
- Bring fresh, community-based examples into national marketing.
- Share success stories internally and with donors/partners.

**Why this matters:** regular contact turns communities into a **continuous source of stories and products**, not just occasional case studies.

---

### 3.3 Identifies Support Needs Before They Become Problems

Communities often won’t reach out until something is already broken – a booking system that doesn’t work, a trail that’s fallen into disrepair, or volunteers who are burned out.

A simple, structured update form creates a space to ask:

- “What support do you need?”
- “Is there anything blocking you right now?”

**Why this matters:** early visibility allows tourism authorities to:

- Provide targeted support before issues escalate.
- Allocate limited resources to where they’re most needed.
- Build stronger, trust-based relationships through responsive follow‑up.

---

### 3.4 Demonstrates a Real Commitment to Inclusive Tourism

Data from the system – showing all communities, across all regions, and their level of engagement – is a powerful way to demonstrate that:

> “Community tourism isn’t a slogan. We are actually talking to communities, regularly, all over the country.”

**Why this matters:**

- Internally: leadership can see clearly where CBT is active and where support is thin.
- Externally: partners and donors can see concrete, consistent engagement with communities.
- Politically: it strengthens the case that CBT should be part of **core tourism strategy**, not a side programme.

## 4. The System Design: Simple, Purposeful, Effective

### 4.1 What It Does

At a high level, the system:

1. **Tracks engagement**  
   Every time staff interact with a community (visit, call, WhatsApp, email), they record a quick update:
   - What happened?
   - What content (if any) was produced?
   - When is the next planned touchpoint?

2. **Calculates status automatically**  
   The system uses dates to show:
   - Communities updated recently (Green),
   - Communities that are going quiet (Yellow),
   - Communities that haven’t been contacted in a long time (Red).

3. **Highlights communities needing attention**  
   Any community that has been quiet for a set period (e.g. **30+ days**) is surfaced, so staff can:
   - Check in,
   - Ask if there is news,
   - Ask if there are questions or support needs.

4. **Provides a clear, shared view for everyone**  
   A simple dashboard shows:
   - All communities,
   - Their last update,
   - Days since last contact,
   - Recent engagement and content counts.

This gives leadership, staff, and partners a **single, shared picture** of how community tourism is doing.

---

## 5. The 30‑Day Rule: A Gentle Check‑In, Not a Punishment

The exact time frame (21 days, 30 days, 45 days) can be adjusted. The important principle is:

> “No community should go too long without being asked how they’re doing.”

Using **30+ days** as a starting point:

- Any community that hasn’t had an update in 30 days is marked for a **gentle check‑in**, not as a failure.
- The message is:  
  “We just want to hear from you. Do you have news? Any questions? Anything we should know?”

This keeps a **low-pressure but consistent** touchpoint between ETA and communities and supports the narrative that CBT communities are central partners, not afterthoughts.

---

## 6. What Changes Day‑to‑Day for Staff

For staff, the system is designed to feel **lightweight**:

- **After a visit or call**: open the form (on phone or laptop) and spend 1–2 minutes:
  - Pick the community,
  - Tick engagement types,
  - Add a short summary,
  - Note any content produced and the next planned visit.

- **Once a week**:
  - Open the dashboard,
  - Look at Red/Yellow communities,
  - Decide which communities to call, message, or visit that week.

- **For reporting**:
  - Use the built‑in PDF export or stats view instead of building reports by hand.

The goal is to **replace scattered notes and memory** with a simple habit:
> “Update the system as soon as you finish an interaction.”

## 7. Driving Adoption When People Don’t Read Emails

We know that “we’ll send an email reminder” on its own won’t change behaviour. Adoption needs to be built into **habits, meetings, and culture**, not just the inbox.

### 7.1 Make the Tool Immediately Useful to Staff

People will use the tool if it helps them, not just head office.

- **Give staff something back**:
  - Monthly PDF summaries they can use in their own reporting.
  - A quick way to see “my communities” and what’s pending.
- **Minimise friction**:
  - Make sure the form works well on phones.
  - Save staff name automatically.
  - Keep the form short and predictable.

### 7.2 Use Meetings as the Anchor, Not Email

Instead of relying on people noticing reminder emails:

- Open the **dashboard at the start of weekly / bi‑weekly team meetings**.
  - Look at Red/Yellow communities together.
  - Agree who will reach out to which communities.
- Celebrate quick wins:
  - “We brought these three communities from Red back to Green.”
  - “Here are two stories that came directly from regular check‑ins.”

This makes the system part of the **team rhythm**, not an extra task.

### 7.3 Use Channels People Already Check (e.g. WhatsApp)

If email isn’t the main channel people pay attention to:

- Share **short WhatsApp nudges**:
  - A screenshot of the dashboard section,
  - A simple message:  
    “These 3 communities haven’t had an update in 30+ days – can we check in this week?”
- Put the **dashboard link and form link** in the team WhatsApp group description so they are always findable.

### 7.4 Light Accountability, Not Heavy Policing

Adoption improves when there is **gentle, consistent accountability**:

- Assign each community to a **named staff member** in the system.
- Once a month, review:
  - “Which communities are consistently Green?”
  - “Where are we struggling, and why?”
- Use this to offer **support**, not blame:
  - Maybe someone’s workload is too high,
  - Maybe a region is hard to reach,
  - Maybe a community needs a different kind of engagement.

The message should be:
> “The system is here to help us not forget anyone, not to punish anyone.”

---

## 8. How This Supports the Bigger CBT Argument

With this system in place, you can back up bigger strategic messages with evidence:

- “We are in regular contact with X communities across Y regions.”
- “Z% of our CBT partners are Green in the last month.”
- “Here are concrete stories and content pieces that came from community check‑ins.”
- “Here’s how many visits, calls, and pieces of content we’ve supported in community tourism this quarter.”

That turns CBT from a **narrative** into a **measurable practice**, and this system becomes a small but important piece of the wider shift:

> From tourism *for* communities  
> to tourism *with* communities.
