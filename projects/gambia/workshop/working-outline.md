## Data Quick Reference

### D1 — Creative Industries Digital Baseline Assessment (Oct 2025)
*61 stakeholders · 7 creative industry sectors · 23 tour operators*

**Sector digital maturity scores:**
| Sector | Score / Level |
|---|---|
| Audiovisual (Film, Photography) | 41% Intermediate+ |
| Marketing / Publishing | 38/70 |
| Fashion & Design | 14/70 |
| Performing & Visual Arts | ~20% |
| Festivals & Cultural Events | Polarised (some strong, most weak) |
| Cultural Heritage Sites | 11% |
| Crafts & Artisan Products | 10% |

**Key headline statistics:**
- **61%** of creative industry stakeholders = Absent/Basic (minimal/no digital footprint)
- **64%** post less than monthly on social media
- Only **16%** have digital booking or payment systems
- Platform governance and continuity identified as systemic gap — many platforms exist but not actively maintained or linked
- Named sector leaders: QTV Gambia · Sawallo Couture · Janjanbureh Kankurang Festival · Janeya Tours · Omi Tours · My Gambia

### D2 — Regional Benchmarking & Market Positioning Analysis (Oct 2025)
*239 tour operator pages · 32 operators · 13 source markets · 5,682 TripAdvisor reviews · 6 West African destinations (Senegal, Cape Verde, Ghana, Nigeria, Benin, The Gambia)*

**Tour operator representation:**
- **95%** of international operator pages include beach/resort framing
- **91%** include wildlife/nature framing
- Only **9%** highlight Gambian creative content — vs 26% Senegal, 21% Ghana
- Creative Tourism Score: **16.9/100** (Gambia) vs regional mean **30.9**
- Pure Gambia tours: CTS 16.9 · Senegal+Gambia packages: CTS **33.9** (more than double)

**Visitor sentiment:**
- The Gambia ranks **#1 regionally on safety perception** — rarely foregrounded in marketing
- Above regional average across most themes; "**liked but not looked for**"
- Weakest theme (relative to own performance): Facilities & Infrastructure
- Tour operators: best digital ambassadors — review sentiment **+0.32**, avg rating **4.7/5**

**Source markets:**
- UK/Anglophone: 45% of reviews, avg rating 4.32/5
- Dutch/Belgian: 34%, avg **4.56/5** — highest satisfaction of any group
- Francophone: 15%, avg 4.06/5 — main barrier: near-total absence of French content
- German/Swiss: 3% (wildlife-focused) · Southern European: 3% (artistic quality)

**Five visitor personas (D2 §5):**
Market Explorers · Heritage Seekers · Learning Travelers · Nature & Wildlife Connectors · Dutch Immersive Explorers

### Opening Activity — Perception Gap (15 min)

**[SLIDE: L9 Exercise]** — "Three words"

Step 1 (individual, 3 min): Write three words you believe international visitors currently use to describe The Gambia as a destination.
Step 2 (individual, 2 min): Write three words you *wish* they used.
Step 3 (pairs, 5 min): Compare. Where do you agree? Where do you diverge?

### Thing 3 — Open one door

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "The door only you can open"
- Individual SMEs — even successful ones — cannot introduce themselves to international tour operators, invite travel journalists, or add themselves to trade fair delegations.
- "You can. You have the mandate, the credibility, and the relationships."
- Three types of door-opening:
  - Access to **buyers** — tour operators, OTAs, trade fairs
  - Access to **audiences** — travel journalists, bloggers, content creators
  - Access to **platforms** — TripAdvisor partnerships, OTA listings, national portals
- **[PHOTO: Professional meeting or handshake — collaborative, not corporate]**

---

**[SLIDE: L8 Case study]** — "What closing the operator gap looks like"
- *D2, §3.4: "To close the gap, Gambia's strategy should prioritise curated storytelling partnerships with operators already active in the region, develop bookable creative-industry products, and provide ready-to-use bilingual marketing materials that translate community-based creativity into itineraries recognizable to global buyers."*
- Three specific institutional actions:
  1. Invite one international operator per year for a familiarisation trip focused on creative industries
  2. Produce a two-page sector brief on each creative sector — for operators to use in their own marketing
  3. Broker two direct introductions between operators and SME producers from the training week

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "What each tier can do"

- **GT Board / Ministry**: operator fam trips, trade fair delegations, OTA destination partnerships
- **NCAC / Hospitality Institute**: cultural sector introductions, journalist hosting, platform partnerships
- **Associations**: member recommendations to operators, peer referrals, directory-driven introductions
- "You don't need to do all three. Identify one door. Open it once. Then make it a habit."

### Thing 4 — Share one briefing

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "Intelligence your members don't know to ask for"
- SMEs know their own businesses. They don't know how The Gambia is being sold internationally, what operators are saying, or what trends are emerging in source markets.
- You do — because you've been monitoring (Thing 1).
- "Translating that into one page, four times a year, is one of the highest-value things you can do for your members."
- **[PHOTO: Person reading a message on a phone — Gambian context, casual]**

---

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "What a good briefing looks like"
A good quarterly briefing has three sections:
1. **What we saw** — key findings from monitoring (2–3 bullet points)
2. **What it means for you** — practical implications for your members
3. **One thing to do** — a specific action members can take this month

Tone: "Here's what we're seeing, and what it might mean for your business." Guidance, not evaluation. Peers sharing intelligence, not management reporting down.
Format: Whatever your members actually read — WhatsApp message, email, agenda item.
Time required: **2 hours per quarter.**

---

**[SLIDE: L10 3-step]** — "From monitoring to briefing"
- Step 1: Check 3–4 sources (Google Alert results, 20 TripAdvisor reviews, 5 operator pages)
- Step 2: Note 2–3 things that are new, changing, or worth acting on
- Step 3: Write one paragraph per finding. Share by WhatsApp or at your next meeting.
- Pink accent: Step 3 — this is where the value is delivered

## BLOCK 4 — TRACK + COORDINATE (9:00–10:30 · 90 min) ⬜

**Objective:** Give participants a simple, free, sustainable monitoring framework. Connect monitoring to coordination — shared data creates the foundation for shared action.

---

**[SLIDE: L2 Section divider]** — "Block 4 · Tracking and Coordination"
- Eyebrow: BLOCK 4
- Sub: "A simple check done consistently is more valuable than a comprehensive study done once every two years."

---

**[SLIDE: L7 Pull quote]** — *"Routine beats sophistication."*
- Sub: "The goal is awareness, not analysis. Three questions. Monthly. Shared."

---

**[SLIDE: L10 3-step]** — "Three questions a monitoring routine answers"
- Are we being found by the right audiences?
- Are the people who visit having the experience we want them to have?
- Are things getting better or worse over time?
- Pink accent: the third question — "over time" is where consistency pays off

---

### A simple monitoring framework

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "Quarterly operator catalogue check"
- Visit the websites of 10 key operators selling The Gambia (same operators each quarter)
- Note: How is The Gambia described? Which experiences are featured? What images are used? Is your sector present?
- Record in a simple shared spreadsheet. Compare quarter-on-quarter.
- *Time required: 2 hours per quarter*

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "Monthly review sentiment scan"
- Read the 20 most recent TripAdvisor reviews for The Gambia destination page and 2–3 key attractions
- Note recurring themes — both positive and negative
- Flag anything requiring a response (below 3 stars, specific complaint, factual error)
- Track direction: improving / stable / declining
- *Time required: 1 hour per month*

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "Annual benchmarking snapshot"
- Once a year: assess The Gambia's digital presence using the same D1 dimensions — social media activity, website currency, discoverability, booking capability
- Compare year-on-year to track progress
- *D1 and D2 baselines provide your Year 1 starting point*

---

- *D2, Table 20: ready-made baseline → target framework*

| Indicator | 2025 Baseline | 2026 Target |
|---|---|---|
| ITO creative tourism visibility | 52% | 60% |
| Average sentiment score | +0.24 | +0.30 |
| Verified TripAdvisor reviews | current | +25% |
| Average review rating | 4.06 | 4.20 |

Pink accent on the target column. "These are ready-made. You don't need to design your own starting framework."

### Monitoring as coordination

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "The value of monitoring together"
- Monitoring done by one institution produces one view.
- Monitoring done by three institutions and shared produces a common picture that no single one could create.
- "The quarterly call is the mechanism. Two to three hours per quarter. GT Board convenes. NCAC, associations, sector bodies share what they've seen."
- *D2, §6.3: Implementation roadmap proposes quarterly partner coordination meetings as a core governance mechanism, with GT Board as lead coordinator and custodian of the Digital Positioning Opportunities Matrix*

---

**[SLIDE: L10 3-step]** — "The quarterly coordination call"
- Step 1: Share — what has each institution observed in the past quarter?
- Step 2: Identify — what's new, changing, or worth acting on together?
- Step 3: Agree — is there one joint response this quarter?
- Pink accent: Step 3

---

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "Who convenes?"
- GT Board: lead coordinator, custodian of shared KPI data
- NCAC + sector associations: core participants
- Ministry of Tourism: invited, not required for every call
- Format: call, brief meeting, or shared document — whatever is **most likely to actually happen**

---

**✦ Activity — Read TripAdvisor live (15 min)** *(replaces knowledge-based digital health check)*

**[SLIDE: L9 Exercise]** — "Read TripAdvisor right now"

Step 1: Open tripadvisor.com → search "The Gambia" → destination page → sort Most Recent
Step 2: Read 10–15 reviews. Note on paper: (a) main positive theme repeated, (b) any complaint or criticism, (c) whether your sector or institution is mentioned
Step 3: Also note overall rating; are there management responses? What do most recent reviews say?

↔ Alternate: If internet unavailable, facilitator projects the TripAdvisor Gambia page — exercise works from the projected screen.

**[SLIDE: L3 Content+image]** — "What did you find?" (share-back)
- Groups of 3 compare notes, then share back
- Main positive theme? Recurring complaint? Sector/institution mentioned?
- What would change if institutions responded to more reviews regularly?
- "This is 15 minutes of exactly what your monthly TripAdvisor scan looks like. The only difference: you take notes and share with members."

*NB: This is the anchor exercise for the Block 5 briefing draft. Participants use what they found here to write their three sentences in Block 5.*

---

**✦ Activity — Draft your monitoring indicators (20 min)**

**[SLIDE: L9 Exercise]** — "Your three indicators"

Each participant (or institution pair) drafts on a printed sheet:
1. Three things they will track *(from the framework above, or adapted for their sector)*
2. How often *(monthly / quarterly / annual)*
3. Who is responsible *(a specific role or name — not "the institution")*
4. How they will share findings *(WhatsApp / email / sector meeting)*

Pairs exchange and offer one piece of feedback: "Is the responsible person specific enough? Are the indicators checkable?"

Brief share-back: what commitments have been made? Where might institutions support each other's monitoring?

↔ Alternate: If participants are from a single institution, produce one shared plan as a group. Assign specific indicators to specific roles within the institution.
