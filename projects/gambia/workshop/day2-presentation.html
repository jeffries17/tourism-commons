Tracking and
Coordination

A simple check done consistently is more valuable than a comprehensive study done once every two years.

"Routine beats sophistication."

The goal is awareness, not analysis.
Three questions. Monthly. Shared.

The monitoring routine

Three questions a monitoring routine answers

1

Are we being found?

By the right audiences, on the platforms they use to plan and book

2

Right experience?

Are visitors having the experience we want them to have and report?

3

Improving over time?

Consistency is where the value builds — over time is where it compounds

These aren't reporting questions. They're the questions that tell you whether your actions are working.

Tool 1 · Quarterly

Operator catalogue check

  • Visit the websites of 10 key operators selling The Gambia — same 10 each quarter
  • Note: How is The Gambia described? Which experiences are featured? What images are used? Is your sector present?
  • Record in a simple shared spreadsheet. Compare quarter-on-quarter.
  • Flag anything that misrepresents The Gambia's offer or excludes your sector
Time required: 2 hours per quarter. One person can run this for all institutions.
What to record

Four questions per operator

How is Gambia described? What's featured? What images? Is your sector there?

What changes over time

Track direction, not perfection

Are descriptions improving? Is creative tourism appearing more? The trend matters more than any single snapshot.

Tool 2 · Monthly

Review sentiment scan

  • Read the 20 most recent TripAdvisor reviews for The Gambia destination page and 2–3 key attractions
  • Note recurring themes — both positive and negative
  • Flag anything requiring a response: below 3 stars, specific complaint, or factual error
  • Track direction: improving / stable / declining
Time required: 1 hour per month. The Gambia's average sentiment score is currently +0.24 — a ready-made baseline to track against.
tripadvisor.com · The Gambia destination
Tool 3 · Annual

Annual benchmarking snapshot

  • Once a year: assess The Gambia's digital presence using the same D1 dimensions — social media activity, website currency, discoverability, booking capability
  • Compare year-on-year to track overall progress across sectors
  • D1 and D2 baselines provide your Year 1 starting point — no need to design from scratch
  • Share the summary across all institutions before the first quarterly call of the year
You are not starting from zero. The assessments are your baseline. Year 1 is already documented.
When things go wrong

Crisis and reputation management

Your monitoring routine will surface problems. Institutions need an agreed protocol for when they do — agreed before a crisis, not during one.

  • Designate one person per institution responsible for weekly monitoring
  • Agree a response threshold — e.g. any review below 3 stars on an official platform
  • Agree tone and messaging guidelines in advance
  • Share patterns of negative reviews at the quarterly call — don't manage them in isolation
google.com/alerts

Google Alerts — free, takes 5 minutes to set up, runs automatically

Platform governance

Who owns what?

Platform governance is an organisational question, not a technical one. For each official platform — website, social media, Google Business profile — who has admin access? Who is responsible for updates? What happens when that person leaves?

Digital orphans

Platforms created for projects that ended without a handover plan — still live, appearing credible to visitors, but containing outdated information. The D1 baseline identified this as a systemic gap.

Starting action: one spreadsheet, one hour. List every official digital touchpoint, note when it was last updated, and identify who has access.

The Gambia's official tourism website — a key touchpoint for every prospective visitor

Closing the loop

From monitoring to SME guidance

Monitoring shared only within institutions has limited value. The goal is to translate intelligence into guidance that SMEs can actually act on.

Q
Quarterly briefing note
One page. What the data shows, what it means for operators. 2 hours to produce — distributed via WhatsApp or email.
!
Short alert
When something significant changes — a new operator selling The Gambia, a cluster of negative reviews. Triggered, not scheduled.
A
Annual shared meeting
Institutions share findings. SMEs respond with ground-level experience. The exchange works in both directions.
Tone: "here is what we are seeing and what it might mean for you" — not evaluation or judgment.

Monitoring as coordination

The value of monitoring together

One institution monitors

Produces one view — partial, limited to one mandate and one sector

Three institutions share

Produces a common picture that no single institution could create alone

Shared picture enables

Shared action — the quarterly call turns observation into coordination

The quarterly call is the mechanism. Two to three hours per quarter. GT Board convenes. NCAC, associations, and sector bodies share what they've seen.

The coordination mechanism

The quarterly coordination call

1

Share

What has each institution observed in the past quarter? One thing each.

2

Identify

What's new, changing, or worth acting on together? Where are the patterns?

3

Agree

Is there one joint response this quarter? Assign it before the call ends.

"Format: a call, a brief meeting, or a shared document — whatever is most likely to actually happen."
Governance

Who convenes?

GT Board
Lead coordinator and custodian of shared KPI data. Convenes the quarterly call. Maintains the monitoring spreadsheet. Distributes the annual benchmarking summary.
NCAC + associations
Core participants. Each brings their sector's observation from the past quarter. Contribute to the shared TripAdvisor scan and operator catalogue check where mandates align.
Ministry of Tourism
Invited, not required for every call. Summary briefing note shared after each quarterly meeting. Engages when a joint response requires policy or budget support.
Start with a small, committed group. The key criterion is not who should be there — it's who will reliably show up.

Activity — Read TripAdvisor right now

15 min
Open TripAdvisor on your phone or laptop. Search: "The Gambia". Go to the destination page. Read 10–15 of the most recent reviews.
  1. 1Go to tripadvisor.com → search "The Gambia" → open the destination page → sort by Most Recent
  2. 2Read 10–15 reviews. On paper, note: (a) the main positive theme you see repeated, (b) any complaint or criticism, (c) whether your sector, institution, or members are mentioned anywhere
  3. 3Also note: what is the overall rating? Are there responses from management to any reviews? What do the most recent reviews say?
↔ If internet is unavailable: the facilitator projects the TripAdvisor Gambia page. Note what you observe from the projected screen — the exercise still works.
Activity share-back

What did you find?

Groups of 3 — compare notes. Then share back to the room:

  • What was the main positive theme repeated across reviews?
  • Was there a recurring complaint or criticism?
  • Was your sector or institution mentioned anywhere?
  • What would change if an institution responded to more reviews regularly?
This is 15 minutes of exactly what your monthly TripAdvisor scan looks like in practice. The only difference: you take notes and share findings with your members.

Activity — Draft your three indicators

20 minutes · Individual then pairs

Each participant drafts on a printed sheet. Be specific — a monitoring plan that can't be assigned to a real person won't happen.

  • 1
    Three things you will track — drawn from the framework above, or adapted for your sector and mandate
  • 2
    How often — monthly, quarterly, or annual
  • 3
    Who is responsible — a specific role or name, not "the institution"
  • 4
    How you will share findings — WhatsApp group, email, sector meeting, or quarterly call
💬

Pairs exchange + brief share-back

10 minutes exchange · 5 minutes share-back

Exchange your draft with a partner from a different institution. Offer one piece of feedback on theirs.

  • 1
    Is the responsible person specific enough? A role or a name — not "GT Board" or "the association"
  • 2
    Are the indicators checkable? You'll know unambiguously whether they happened
  • 3
    Is the sharing channel realistic? Something that will actually happen, not something aspirational
↔ If participants are from a single institution: produce one shared plan as a group. Assign specific indicators to specific roles within the institution.
