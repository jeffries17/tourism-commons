# North Star Checklist — Lo Responsable Vende
## Audience Alignment Reference · Módulo 1 Online (Sessions 1 & 2)
*Use this checklist to audit any slide, exercise, or talking point before Monday.*

---

## Who is in the room

**31 participants** — all Colombian, almost all based in Medellín / Antioquia.
Sectors: ~40% tour operators, ~25% travel agencies, ~20% hotels/lodging, ~15% other (transport, wellness, ecopark).
Company size: ~75% have 1–10 employees. Two outliers at 50+ (Tequendama Hotel, Inversiones AVIOA). Many are the founder AND the sales person AND the operations person.
Experience with European market: very limited — almost none state prior international commercial experience.
Core identity: they overwhelmingly describe themselves as sustainable, responsible, community, regenerative, or nature-based. This is their point of pride.
Primary stated need (from registrations): access European buyers. Secondary: learn how to tell their story and structure their offer.

## The checklist

Apply each check to every major section of the deck. Mark ✓ / ✗ / ⚠ (partial).

---

### A · Audience fit

**A1 — Is this written for a micro-business owner, not a corporate team?**
Most participants run 1–10 person operations. Many are the sole commercial contact. Content that assumes a "marketing department", a "sales team", or dedicated budget for digital tools will land poorly.
*Test: read the slide and ask — could Catalina from Ecourabaes (1 person, Urabá) do this alone?*

**A2 — Does it assume zero prior international market experience?**
Participants want to reach Europe but most haven't done it yet. Terms like "B2B funnel", "ITO", "FIT operator", "trade fair pipeline" need to be introduced, not assumed.
*Evidence: "vender al mercado europeo" is the #1 goal — it's aspirational, not current reality for most.*

**A3 — Does it acknowledge the diversity of sectors in the room?**
A hotel manager (Cannúa, Tequendama) has different levers than a community tour guide (Historias de mi Barrio) or a travel agency (TMS, Colombia Inspira). Examples and exercises should flex across at least two of these archetypes, or prompt participants to apply to their own type.
*Risk: defaulting to "tour operator" examples alienates the hotel and agency participants (~45% of the room).*

**A4 — Does it respect that many participants are also just learning to use digital tools?**
Several participants described basic goals ("aparecer en Europa", "tener visibilidad"). Assume moderate, not advanced, digital literacy. Avoid implying that not having a polished website or a CRM means they're behind — frame it as a starting point.

---

### B · European market grounding

**B1 — Is the European buyer concrete and specific, not generic?**
The three buyer profiles (specialist agency, mainstream TO with green line, FIT operator) are the backbone of Session 1. Each slide about "the European buyer" should clearly state which profile it's talking about — or it will feel abstract and theoretical.
*Test: does this slide name a buyer type? If it says "the European buyer wants X" without specifying who, flag it.*

**B2 — Does it show what actually closes a deal (not just what sounds impressive)?**
Key insight from the program: Europeans don't always want the certification — they want the verifiable story behind it. Participants who have authentic community relationships, local guides, and documented impact can compete even without formal certification. This is a confidence-builder and a strategic reframe.
*Evidence: John Fredy (Amanecer, Chocó) is a responsible operator without formal certs. He needs to know that's enough to pitch.*

**B3 — Does it bridge their sustainability identity to commercial language?**
Most participants describe their work in internal/ethical terms ("turismo comunitario", "prácticas responsables", "turismo de consciencia"). The sessions need to give them the commercial translation — what does "trabajamos con guías locales" sound like when you pitch it to a Lyon agency?
*The gap: they know their value. They don't know how to say it to someone who buys 200 trips a year.*

**B4 — Does it make the European market feel reachable, not aspirational?**
Several participants wrote things like "ampliar nuestros clientes" or "crecer" — the market feels distant. Concrete mechanics (where buyers look, what they evaluate, how long decision cycles take) make it tangible. Avoid framing Europe as a dream; frame it as a system that has a door they can find.

---

### C · Practical value

**C1 — Does every block end with something they can DO, not just know?**
This group registered because they want commercial results. Each content block should close with a specific action, template, or exercise — not a summary of concepts. The Hoja de Mapeo de Nicho in Session 1 and the Auditoría in Session 2 are the right model.
*Test: after this slide/section, what does the participant write down, fill in, or do differently this week?*

**C2 — Are examples from companies their size and type?**
Examples from large Colombian operators or international hotel chains will feel irrelevant. Prioritize small/micro operators from Latin America — ideally from Colombia, or from neighboring countries with similar profiles. If citing a larger example, explicitly note why the lesson applies to a 3-person operation.
*Participants who will particularly scan for relevance: Ecourabaes (1 person, Urabá), Mundos Paisa Tours (1 person), Corporación amigos del bosque andino (1 person, nature guiding).*

**C3 — Is language accessible? No unexplained jargon.**
Anglicisms and sector acronyms need to be introduced before they're used. This applies especially to: B2B, FIT, ITO, CRM, KPI, SEM, SEO. The sessions are in Spanish — use Spanish framing first, then introduce the English term if needed.
*The audience has strong sector knowledge of Colombian tourism. Their gap is in international market vocabulary, not tourism fundamentals.*

**C4 — Does the session respect 2-hour online attention limits?**
Sessions run 9:00–11:00 Medellín (late afternoon in Paris for the European facilitator). Participants are joining from their offices or homes. Content-heavy blocks need to be broken by interaction every 10–15 minutes. Check that each block has a moment for chat, poll, breakout, or exercise — not sustained presentation.

---

### D · Storytelling and narrative

**D1 — Does it address the "how to tell my story" gap?**
This was the most nuanced and recurring theme in the registration motivations. Participants like Ecourabaes wrote explicitly: "sentimos que es un producto turístico muy único y ganador pero requerimos focalizar nuestro esfuerzo de marketing y comercial." They know their story is powerful — they don't know how to package it for buyers who see hundreds of operators.
*This gap is more urgent than any technical skill gap. It should be named directly in Session 1.*

**D2 — Does it show that authenticity IS the commercial advantage?**
Several participants are clearly self-conscious about what they don't have (certifications, big websites, English-speaking teams). The sessions should reframe: operators with genuine community relationships, local knowledge, and documented impact have something many large operators cannot replicate. This is not consolation — it's a market positioning fact.
*Use this to open Session 1 Block 1, not bury it at the end.*

---

### E · Confidence and tone

**E1 — Does it validate before it challenges?**
Most participants are coming with real operations and real pride in what they do. Opening with what European buyers want (without first acknowledging what participants already have) can feel like criticism. The arc should be: *you have something valuable → here's the language and the channel to reach buyers who want it.*

**E2 — Does it avoid implying they're behind or broken?**
Framing like "the mistake most operators make" or "why Colombian tourism is invisible to Europeans" needs to be handled carefully. Use it to create urgency, not shame. The participants who wrote "para ser competitivo" (José, Hostal Pura Vida) or "estar al nivel de la competencia" are already self-aware — they don't need more doubt, they need a clear path.

**E3 — Does it give participants something to say to each other?**
Breakout exercises (Session 1 Block 2) and live audits (Session 2 Block 2) only work if participants feel safe sharing. Exercises that have a right/wrong answer will shut down honest sharing. Frame every exercise as exploration, not evaluation.

---

## Quick scan (3-question version)

When reviewing any single slide or section:

1. **Who is this for?** → Can a 1–5 person sustainable tourism micro-business in Medellín immediately apply this?
2. **What do they do with it?** → Is there a concrete next action, tool, or language shift?
3. **Does it honor their identity?** → Does it treat their sustainability and community work as a commercial asset, not just a nice ethical feature?

If the answer to any of the three is no or unclear, the slide needs revision before Monday.
